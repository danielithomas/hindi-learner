# Liking & Preferences — The Dative Pattern (मुझे पसंद)

**Date:** 2026-03-14
**Topic:** Expressing likes and preferences in Hindi — why "I like" becomes "to me, it pleases", the dative construction with "mujhe", and superlatives with "sabse accha"
**Context:** Discussing which items from a creative project were favourites — practising opinions and preferences in natural conversation.

---

## The Practice

**Learner's attempts and corrections:**

1. ❌ "Mein kuch wallpapers pur pasand houn" → ✅ "**Mujhe** kuch wallpapers **pasand aaye**" (I liked some of the wallpapers)
2. ❌ "Mein abstract wallpapers ko pasand sabse acche houn" → ✅ "**Mujhe** abstract wallpapers **sabse acche lage**" (I liked the abstract wallpapers the best)

## Core Concept: The Dative "Liking" Pattern

**English thinks:** "I like something" (I = subject, something = object)
**Hindi thinks:** "To me, something is pleasing" (something = subject, to me = receiver)

This is why **mein** (I) becomes **mujhe** (to me) when expressing likes:

| English | Hindi (Wrong ❌) | Hindi (Correct ✅) | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| I like this | Mein yeh pasand hoon | **Mujhe yeh pasand hai** | [🔊](https://translate.google.com/?sl=hi&tl=en&text=%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%9D%E0%A5%87%20%E0%A4%AF%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B9%20%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A6%20%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%88&op=translate) |
| I liked this | Mein pasand kiya | **Mujhe yeh pasand aaya** | [🔊](https://translate.google.com/?sl=hi&tl=en&text=%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%9D%E0%A5%87%20%E0%A4%AF%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B9%20%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A6%20%E0%A4%86%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE&op=translate) |
| I liked these (plural) | — | **Mujhe yeh pasand aaye** | [🔊](https://translate.google.com/?sl=hi&tl=en&text=%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%9D%E0%A5%87%20%E0%A4%AF%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B9%20%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A6%20%E0%A4%86%E0%A4%AF%E0%A5%87&op=translate) |

## Common Mistakes (Learner Patterns)

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **Mein** pasand hoon | "I am a preference" — makes you the preference! | **Mujhe** pasand hai |
| Mujhe pasand **kiya** | "kiya" = actively did — but liking isn't an action you do | Mujhe pasand **aaya** |
| wallpapers **ko** pasand | Unnecessary "ko" — the thing liked is the subject | wallpapers pasand aaye |
| sabse acche **houn** | "houn" = I am — but we need "felt/seemed" | sabse acche **lage** |

## Practice Exercises

Try expressing these preferences in Hindi:

1. I liked the movie → ?
2. I liked the coffee the most → ?
3. Which one did you like? → ?
4. I need water → ?
5. I think this is good → ?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Mujhe film pasand aayi (aayi = feminine past, because "film" is feminine)
2. Mujhe coffee sabse acchi lagi (acchi/lagi = feminine because "coffee" is feminine)
3. Tumhe kaun sa pasand aaya?
4. Mujhe paani chahiye
5. Mujhe lagta hai yeh accha hai

</details>

## Key Takeaway

**The "mujhe" pattern is one of the most important in Hindi.** It's used for:
- Liking (pasand)
- Needing (chahiye)
- Knowing (pata hai)
- Feeling (lagta hai)

Master this dative pattern and a huge chunk of everyday Hindi opens up. Think of it as "to me, X happens" rather than "I do X."
