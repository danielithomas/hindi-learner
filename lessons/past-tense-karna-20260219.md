# Past Tense with Karna (करना) — To Do/Work

**Date:** 2026-02-19
**Level:** Beginner
**Topic:** Using "kaam karna" (to work) in different tenses, and the ergative case with "maine"

---

## Key Vocabulary

| Hindi (Romanised) | Devanagari | English |
|-------------------|-----------|---------|
| kaam | काम | work (noun) |
| karna | करना | to do |
| kiya | किया | did (past) |
| karunga | करूँगा | I will do (masc.) |
| karenge | करेंगे | we will do / formal I will do |
| kar raha hoon | कर रहा हूँ | I am doing (present continuous, masc.) |
| kar raha tha | कर रहा था | I was doing (past continuous, masc.) |
| sahi | सही | correct / right |
| bol raha tha | बोल रहा था | was saying (past continuous, masc.) |
| bolta | बोलता | speaks (habitual) |
| seekha | सीखा | learned (past) |
| kuch naya | कुछ नया | something new |

---

## Core Pattern: Kaam + Karna

In Hindi, "kaam" (work) is a noun. To turn it into a verb ("to work"), pair it with **karna** (to do):

| Hindi | English | Tense |
|-------|---------|-------|
| kaam karunga | I will work | Future |
| kaam kar raha hoon | I am working | Present continuous |
| kaam kar raha tha | I was working | Past continuous |
| kaam kiya | I worked / I did work | Simple past |

---

## The Ergative Case: Mein → Maine

When using **kiya** (did) in the past tense, the subject changes from **mein** (I) to **maine** (मैंने):

- ✅ **Maine** kaam kiya (I did work)
- ✅ **Mein** kaam kar raha hoon (I am working)

**Rule of thumb:**
- Present/future → **mein**
- Past tense with "kiya" → **maine**

---

## Common Mistakes & Fixes

### ❌ "Mein kaam tha"
- Reads as: "I was work" (kaam as noun, no verb)
- Fix: "Mein kaam **kar raha tha**" (I was working)

### ❌ "Mein kaam kar**enge**"
- "Karenge" = we will do (plural/formal)
- For informal "I will do": "Mein kaam kar**unga**" (masculine)
- Note: "karenge" is acceptable in formal/professional Hindi for "I"

### ❌ "Kya mein bolta kaam the"
- Word order and tense mismatch
- Fix: "Kya mein sahi **bol raha tha**?" (Was I saying it correctly?)
- "bolta" = habitual, "bol raha tha" = was saying (past continuous)
- "the" → "tha" (singular masculine for "I")

---

## Example Sentences

| Hindi | English |
|-------|---------|
| Aaj maine kaam kiya | Today I worked |
| Kal mein kaam karunga | Tomorrow I will work |
| Mein abhi kaam kar raha hoon | I am working right now |
| Kya mein sahi bol raha tha? | Was I saying it correctly? |
| Aaj maine kuch naya seekha | Today I learned something new |

---

## Notes

- **Karna** (करना) is one of the most important verbs in Hindi — it turns many nouns into actions (kaam karna = to work, madad karna = to help, pyaar karna = to love)
- The ergative **maine** pattern applies whenever you use a transitive verb in simple past tense — not just kiya
- Tenses were practised through self-correction: noun-only → past continuous → simple past. Building sentences from scratch is a sign of real progress.
